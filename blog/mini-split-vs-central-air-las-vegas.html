Mini-Split vs Central Air in Las Vegas: Which Is Better?

Ductless mini-splits and central air both cool Las Vegas homes. Here is how they differ and where each one fits.

VVegas Cool AC RepairOctober 20263 min read

Central air and ductless mini-splits are two common ways to cool a home. Neither is better in every situation, so the right choice depends on your home and how you use it.

How Central Air Works

A central system cools air at one indoor unit and sends it through ductwork to vents in each room. It is a good fit for homes that already have well-built ducts and want one thermostat controlling the whole house.

How a Mini-Split Works

A mini-split has an outdoor unit connected to one or more wall-mounted indoor heads by refrigerant lines. There are no ducts, and each indoor head can be controlled on its own.

Where Each One Fits

  • Central air: whole-home cooling with existing ductwork and a single control.
  • Mini-split: additions, garages, casitas and converted rooms where running ducts is difficult.
  • Mini-split: homes where only certain rooms need extra cooling.
  • Both: some homes use a central system with mini-splits for problem rooms.

Things to Consider

  • Ductwork condition. Leaky ducts waste cooled air. Ductless systems avoid that, and central systems benefit from air duct repair when ducts are leaking.
  • Zoning. Mini-splits let you cool only the rooms you use.
  • Appearance. Mini-splits have visible indoor heads on the wall, which some homeowners prefer to hide.
  • Upfront cost. Costs vary with the size of the home and the number of zones, so ask for a written estimate.

Frequently Asked Questions

1. Is a mini-split better than central air? Neither is better in every case. Mini-splits suit rooms without ductwork, while central air suits homes with good ducts.

2. Can a mini-split cool a whole house? A multi-zone system can cover a whole house. A single-zone system is usually used for one room or open area.

3. Do mini-splits need ductwork? No. They use refrigerant lines between the outdoor unit and the indoor heads.

4. Can I use both in one home? Yes. Some homes use central air with mini-splits for additions or rooms that are hard to cool.
